## The ownership chain (this is worth stating precisely)

**Alphaliner is published by AXSMarine, and Signal Ocean acquired AXSMarine in January 2026.**
Ownership was transferred by shipbroking group **BRS**, which in exchange **took an equity stake in
Signal Ocean and a seat on its board.** The deal folded **AXSDry, Alphaliner and voyage software**
into Signal Ocean's portfolio, along with ERP developers Next Voyage Maritime and Shiplex.

**The enlarged group supports 1,500+ clients** across shipping and commodities. AXSMarine continues
to operate under existing leadership, with integration running through 2026.

So Alphaliner is a **recently acquired asset**, roughly seven months into integration at the time of
writing — which matters for how any of the research below could actually be applied.

## ⚠️ Alphaliner is structurally FreightWaves — the model already exists inside the company

This is the finding that makes it worth studying. Compare:

| | **FreightWaves** (Craig Fuller) | **Alphaliner / Signal Ocean** |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Freight & logistics | Container / liner shipping |
| Positioning | "The Bloomberg of freight" | "The global reference in liner shipping" |
| Core asset | High-frequency proprietary data | Decades-deep proprietary liner database + AIS |
| Media role | Editorial drives inbound for the data business | Publications sold as *"essential market information"* alongside the platform |
| Audience | Supply-chain professionals with budget | Carriers, brokers, financiers, analysts |

Fuller's central claim was that **FreightWaves built its data business to ~$10M ARR with zero
third-party paid advertising**, entirely on inbound from its own media — and that the media
contribution margin effectively pays for customer acquisition ("negative CAC").

**Signal Ocean owns the same two ingredients.** Whether it runs the same play is exactly what the
user can find out and I cannot.

It also sits precisely where [L2](../../LEARNINGS.md) predicts high value: readers are carriers,
brokers, charterers and financiers making eight- and nine-figure decisions, and the vendors who want
to reach them have real budgets. **Commercial intent is about as high as it gets.**

## What is publicly observable

- **Cadence:** weekly newsletter plus monthly publications.
- **Content:** salient industry developments, corporate news, carrier financial results, new services,
  fleet analysis, orderbook, market evolution and statistics.
- **Distribution:** sold/accessed through the AXSMarine platform rather than a standalone consumer
  subscription flow — i.e. **it reads as a product feature, not a standalone media brand.**
- **Sampling:** a full sample issue is published as a public PDF — a classic B2B lead magnet.
- **Longevity:** Alphaliner is a long-established reference in liner shipping, i.e. exactly the kind
  of **long-tenured audience with deep subject attachment** that Fuller argued was systematically
  underpriced.

## ⚠️ What I could not find, and what only the user can answer

These are the questions that would make this the most valuable case study in the project. **Every
other source gives us self-reported numbers from someone with an incentive to flatter them. Here the
real numbers exist internally.**

1. **How many subscribers, and who are they?** Job titles and companies matter more than the count.
2. **What is the pricing and packaging?** Bundled with the platform, or separately sold? Is there a
   free tier feeding a paid one?
3. **Open and engagement rates** — against the ~50% benchmark that Morning Brew and The Rundown claim
   for good B2B/professional lists.
4. **Is revenue attributed to it at all?** Is Alphaliner a P&L line, or a marketing cost centre? This
   single answer determines whether the Fuller playbook is even applicable.
5. **Does it carry advertising?** Given [L4](../../LEARNINGS.md) — the advertiser outbids the reader
   for professional audiences — and vendors who want to reach carriers and brokers, **an unmonetized
   ad slot in front of this audience would be the clearest gap.**
6. **Does it drive platform inbound?** The FreightWaves claim in one number: what share of platform
   trials or demos originate from the newsletter?
7. **What adjacent products could be sold to this audience?** ([L6](../../LEARNINGS.md)) Events,
   research reports, benchmarking, recruitment — Morning Brew found only ~3 of ~12 B2B ad products
   were newsletter placements.

## What's useful for this project

**1. It's a working, real-world instance of the model the research keeps converging on.** High
commercial intent (D1), a professional audience, media attached to a data business, an owned
long-tenured list. **The user can test the project's central conclusions against a real P&L instead
of against podcast anecdotes.**

**2. It's the one place to check [L14](../../LEARNINGS.md) honestly.** Every case study we've
studied had an unpriced advantage. **Working inside a maritime data group *is* that kind of
advantage** — domain access, proprietary data, an existing audience, and colleagues who are the
target reader. Worth being explicit that this is a genuine edge in the Track A assessment.

**3. ⚠️ But it is also a constraint, not just an asset.** Alphaliner is a company asset, recently
acquired and mid-integration. **Anything the user learns here informs the personal project; it does
not become the personal project** unless that's separately agreed. The two should be kept clearly
distinct — including in this repo.

**4. The most likely applicable finding is the advertising one.** If Alphaliner reaches decision-
makers at carriers and brokers and does not sell advertising or sponsored research, that is the gap
[L4](../../LEARNINGS.md) and [L5](../../LEARNINGS.md) predict — priced on advertiser ROI rather than
CPM, with inventory deliberately constrained.

  independent, and the summary should not be read as a neutral assessment of a competitor's asset.
