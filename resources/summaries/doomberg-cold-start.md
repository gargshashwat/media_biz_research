## What it claims

A pseudonymous publication launched with **no social following of any kind** became the largest
finance newsletter on Substack in roughly two years, on organic distribution only, and monetized
nothing for its entire first year.

## Specific numbers and facts

- **First Substack piece: 3 May 2021.**
- Began as an **anonymous publishing arm of an existing content-creation consulting business** — a
  consulting *client* suggested they build their own content rather than only ghostwrite others'.
- **First 12 months: 105 articles**, "many tens of thousands" of subscribers, millions of views.
- **The green chicken avatar was an explicit growth hack.** They had no social presence whatsoever,
  so the memorable identity was built deliberately to be recognizable in a Twitter feed.
- **Stated "Magic Metric": Twitter impressions per day.** Their funnel had three nodes —
  **Twitter followers → free subscribers → paying subscribers.**
- **Ran entirely free for a full year** before launching paid, **explicitly against Substack's own
  advice.** Consulting income removed the pressure to monetize early.
- **Pricing was set by survey, not intuition:** they analysed the **top 40 finance newsletters**,
  took the median, and landed on **$300/yr**, with a **"Doomberg Pro" tier at $1,200/yr.**
- **~200,000 Twitter followers within under two years**; ~**300,000 total subscribers** since launch;
  **tens of thousands of paid subscribers** by 2023-24; reported **seven-figure subscription revenue**.
- **~100 podcast appearances per year.** Described as deliberate: an active Twitter presence coupled
  with appearing on as many podcasts as possible.
- Published a monthly **"The Work of My Life"** series through year one that openly reported growth
  metrics — new Twitter followers and new subscribers each month.

### ⚠️ Then they left X — August 2023

Doomberg put their X account into "lurker mode" and moved their distribution effort to Substack Notes.

> *"The mere mention of 'Substack' in a tweet leads to substantial de-boosting, and we suspect many
> accounts closely associated with the site are being punished by the algorithm."*

They describe having **"built this franchise at the nexus between Old Twitter and Substack"** — and
concluded that X had come to operate against their interests while Substack's incentives were aligned
with theirs.

## What's useful for this project

**1. It is the cleanest cold-start case in the whole project.** No prior following, no name, no
network — which makes it the counterexample to [L14](../../LEARNINGS.md) that the research has been
missing. ⚠️ **But the unpriced advantage is still there, just relocated:** they were already
professional content operators with a *paying consulting business* funding the runway. The writing
skill and the income were both pre-existing.

**2. Two distribution mechanics, and neither is daily engagement.** A recognizable brand identity
carrying the impressions, and **~100 podcast appearances a year** — borrowed audiences at industrial
scale. This is [L16](../../LEARNINGS.md) (cold outreach) executed as a distribution system.

**3. A year free, deliberately, in the highest-paying vertical.** Directly relevant to
[C1](../../LEARNINGS.md) — adds a fifth answer to "when to charge," and it is the one closest to the
staged model in [05-strategy.md](../../research/05-strategy.md).

**4. Pricing by competitive survey.** They read the top 40 finance newsletters and took the median.
Cheap, repeatable, and better than guessing.

**5. ⚠️ The platform-tax finding is the most operationally important thing here.** The single best
documented finance-newsletter operator on X concluded the platform was actively taxing the handoff
from rented attention to owned audience — the exact handoff [L7](../../LEARNINGS.md) says the whole
business depends on.

## Caveats

- **⚠️ No primary source was readable.** Egress policy blocked newsletter.doomberg.com,
  podcastnotes.org and on.substack.com. Treat every figure as indicative.
- **⚠️ Anonymous self-report.** There is no named party accountable for the numbers and no way to
  audit them. This is the weakest evidential position of any case in the project.
- **Not a solo operator, and not a part-time one.** Doomberg is a *team* behind one identity, staffed
  out of an existing agency. ~100 podcasts/year alone would exhaust a 5-10 hr/week budget
  ([L15](../../LEARNINGS.md)). **The mechanics are informative; the capacity is not comparable.**
- **⚠️ Timing.** The 2021-2023 window was "Old Twitter" by their own description, plus an energy
  crisis that made their beat the biggest story in markets. Both conditions are gone.
- **Their X exit is one publisher's read**, made in 2023, and is partly a bet on a competing platform
  whose team they call their partner. Interest is not neutral.
