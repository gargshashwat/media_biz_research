## Your notes from ~2025 (preserved)

> - He wrote 1-2 times a month on Substack. Only posts, no Notes. Grew to 12k subscribers and 14k ARR in 6 months
> - Had a strategy around **FOMO**:
>   - publish my full post on Substack but **future-date it 24+ hours after my social media post**
>   - post my content on Twitter or LinkedIn, drop the link to my future-dated Substack post **in the first comment**, and wait. When users clicked through, they couldn't access the article yet. Substack prompted them to **subscribe to be notified** when it went live in 24+ hours.
>   - Even though the article was often free, **Substack's built-in upsell during the subscription flow naturally captured solid paid conversions.**
> - Quality > quantity, long form content directly relevant to his audience's current challenges
>   - **Substance drives conversions, not consistency for consistency's sake.**

## What's useful for this project

**1. ⚠️ This is the only source arguing *against* cadence, and it matters given our constraint.**
Every other case pushes frequency: Lenny weekly, Poyar's weekly inflection, Morning Brew daily,
Sam Parr 10 articles a week. **Shi claims 12,000 subscribers in 6 months at 1-2 posts a month.**

If true, that is the most capacity-compatible result in the entire research — roughly one deep piece
a fortnight. **It also aligns with the depth-over-volume finding from Poyar, Lenny, Dwarkesh, Senra
and beehiiv.** Treat it as a hypothesis worth testing, not a proven counterexample: one
unverified self-report against several better-sourced cases.

**2. The future-dating mechanic is clever and specific.** Post socially, link to a Substack piece
that isn't live yet, and the platform converts the click into a subscription instead of a read.
**It's the only tactic in the research that turns a distribution click into an owned-audience
capture by default** rather than hoping the reader subscribes after reading.

**3. Free content can still convert paid** via the subscription flow's built-in upsell — the paywall
isn't the only conversion surface. Consistent with the beehiiv finding that top performers treat
onboarding as the conversion moment.

## Caveats

- **⚠️ Source largely inaccessible.** Only the first line is verified; the tactics are from the
  user's notes, one step removed.
- **⚠️ Topic unknown.** I could not confirm what the newsletter is actually about, which makes the
  result hard to interpret — **12,000 subscribers in a hot niche and in a cold one mean very
  different things.**
- **Small absolute numbers.** "5-figure ARR" could be $10k; the user's notes say $14k. That is a
  side-project outcome, not a business — useful as a mechanism study, not a destination.
- **⚠️ The future-dating tactic depends on Substack-specific behaviour** that can change without
  notice, and arguably degrades the reader experience (you click a link and can't read the thing).
  Worth weighing against the trust-building argument every other source makes.
- **Existing audience unaccounted for.** Henry Shi is a known founder with a substantial existing
  following; 12,000 subscribers in 6 months is not a cold start. **The notes don't capture this and
  it's probably the biggest single factor.**
