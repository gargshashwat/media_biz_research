# The State of Paid Newsletters 2026

> **Topic:** **Not a case study — a benchmark report.** First-party platform data on paid newsletter
> **pricing, conversion, churn, and LTV, broken down by vertical**, from thousands of publications on
> beehiiv. The first source giving us base rates rather than one operator's story.

- **Source:** beehiiv Blog — authors Tyler Denk (CEO/co-founder, beehiiv) and Kanishka
- **Type:** Industry benchmark report / first-party data study
- **Link:** https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026
- **Published:** 2026-06-22
- **Consumed:** 2026-08-25
- **Trust:** **medium for the numbers, low for the framing.** The underlying data is real, large-sample, first-party, and comes with a stated methodology — far better evidence than anything we've studied. But **beehiiv sells the exact product this report recommends**, every featured publisher is a beehiiv customer, and the piece repeatedly pitches beehiiv's 0% revenue share against "predatory" competitors. Read the benchmarks; discount the conclusions.
- **⚠️ Third consecutive beehiiv-connected source** (the Imran and Poyar interviews were on beehiiv's podcast). We are over-indexed on one company's view of this industry.

> **Feeds:** Phase 2 (monetization) primarily, Phase 1 and Phase 4 secondarily.

---

## What it claims

Paid subscriptions are the fastest-growing newsletter revenue stream, and **the vertical you choose
determines your ceiling far more than the size of your list.** The gap between median and top-decile
performance is enormous across every metric, and the report attributes that gap to execution rather
than audience size or luck.

---

## Specific numbers and facts

### Platform-level growth
- Paid subscription revenue on beehiiv: **$8M (2024) → $19M (2025), +138%.** Projected **$35M by
  end of 2026**.
- Share of revenue-generating beehiiv users earning through subscriptions: **15% (Q1 2024) → 30%
  (Q1 2026).**
- **Annual billing overtook monthly in mid-2025** (monthly had been ~70% of subscription revenue in
  early 2025).

### Pricing — the market has an anchor and it hasn't moved
- **Median: $10/month, $100/year.** Unchanged since at least early 2024. The 10x annual convention
  holds across nearly every tier.
- **List size does not affect price.** A 500-subscriber publication charges roughly what a
  100,000-subscriber one does. Size only shows up in one-time digital product pricing.

| Vertical | Median $/month |
|---|---|
| **Investing** | **$27** |
| Finance | $20 |
| Business | $15 |
| Lifestyle | $9 |
| **Travel** | **$7** |

Annual: **Investing $292 median, $600 at the top quartile.** Travel $80 median, $131 top quartile.

### Conversion — the median is brutal, the spread is enormous
- **Median free-to-paid conversion: 0.62%** — about 6 paying subscribers per 1,000.
- **Investing:** 0.84% median → **18.69% top decile (22x)**
- **Finance:** 0.78% median → **20.00% top decile (26x)**
- **Sports:** 1.93% median — 3x the platform average, attributed to tribal loyalty
- Top-decile range across verticals: **Technology 5.55% (lowest) → Economy 30.80% (highest)**
- Realistic long-term target: **2-5%** (roughly the top quartile)
- Publishers who "just put some posts behind a paywall" sit around **0.3%**; intentional programs
  reach **5%+**

*Their own caveat:* sports has only 158 paid publications vs 630+ in business — thin verticals may
skew high through self-selection.

### Churn and subscriber lifetime — a 3x spread
| Vertical | Monthly churn | Implied lifetime |
|---|---|---|
| Food & Drink | 5.06% | ~19.8 months |
| News | 5.47% | ~18.3 months |
| Sports | 7.76% | ~12.9 months |
| Investing | 11.72% | ~8.5 months |
| **AI** | **13.33%** | **~7.5 months** |
| Money | 16.67% | ~6 months |

**5% monthly churn → ~54% survive a year. 17% → ~11% survive.** The compounding is the whole story.

**⚠️ AI is flagged by the report as one of the leakiest verticals** despite high interest — the space
moves fast and free alternatives appear constantly, so a paid tier erodes into optional. Investing
converts and prices best but retains only middling: readers buy for a specific window (earnings
season, a market event) and cancel.

### Lifetime value
- **Median LTV per paid subscriber: $83 (Community) to $230 (Investing).**
- Worked examples from the report:
  - 5,000-subscriber **investing** newsletter at median conversion (0.84%) and $27/mo → **~$13,600/yr**
  - 5,000-subscriber **travel** newsletter at 0.30% and $7/mo → **~$1,260/yr**
  - **Same list size, ~11x revenue difference.**
  - 1,000-subscriber investing newsletter → **$2,700+/yr**; 1,000-subscriber travel → **~$252/yr**
  - 1,000 subscribers at platform median (0.62%, $10/mo) → **~$62/month**

### Timing and launch mechanics
- **Median creator launches a paid tier 45 days (~6 weeks) after starting the newsletter.**
- Their argument for not waiting: every week without a paid option anchors the audience to "free"
  and makes the eventual ask harder.
- Those who launch in week one are typically **migrating an audience they already built elsewhere.**

### Tactics the report claims are underused
- **Free and paid should be different products, not the same product with a gate.** Gating a
  fraction of existing free content is "a tax on your most loyal readers." Paid should be deeper
  analysis, exclusive data, community, or direct access.
- **Offer annual from day one** at a 15-20% discount — it removes 11 of 12 "should I cancel?"
  decision points.
- **Build onboarding before launching.** The first 48 hours are the value-confirmation window.
- **A community layer creates switching costs** content alone can't: cancelling becomes *leaving*.
- **Cancel flows** (survey, pause option, targeted offer) recover **10-20%** of attempted cancels.
  Very few publishers run one.
- **Dunning on failed payments** recovers **20-40%** — "the closest thing to free money."
- **No evidence that launching paid reduced engagement among free subscribers.** Fear of alienating
  the free list is, they argue, mostly in publishers' heads.

### Operator examples cited
- **Matt Brown, *Extra Points*** — the business of college sports. **69,000 subscribers, 2,000
  paying (~2.9%), $200k+/year.** Adds university licensing deals and digital products. A very close
  structural analogue to a narrow B2B niche.
- **Oliver Darcy, *Status*** — media industry news, 7 days/week. **Weekdays paywalled, weekends
  free.** Went solo → hired reporters, an editor, and a CRO, funded entirely by subscribers.
- **Lachlan Cartwright, *Breaker Media*** — profitable within a year. Never gives away more than the
  first half-dozen paragraphs; deliberate cliffhanger placement.
- **L.A. Material** — LA local news, launched spring 2026. More subscribers took the **$405/year**
  tier than expected for the whole first year, majority annual. Built membership around forums and
  events *instead of* paywalling investigations.
- **Dr. Jen Ashton, *Ajenda*** — wellness. 181,000+ free subscribers; **six months of content with
  no sales pitch** before launching. A cohort program drew 5,000+ people and **$750K in 90 days with
  zero ad spend**; now a $29/month membership with 13,000+ members. Her stated lesson: *"Information
  is everywhere, but belonging somewhere is rare."* Also that **price filters for commitment** —
  higher investment correlated with higher engagement.
- **The Nerve** — UK journalist-owned. **All journalism free**; members pay to fund the mission.
  **1,000 paying members in week one, ~12% conversion** — driven largely by co-founder Carole
  Cadwalladr's existing audience.

### Stated methodology
First-party data, thousands of beehiiv publications, 2021-2026. Medians throughout; "top 25%/10%"
are the 75th/90th percentiles. **Estimated months = 1 ÷ monthly churn; LTV = median monthly price ×
estimated months** — the report calls these "simplified steady-state estimates based on average
monthly cancellation rates, not cohort survival curves."

## What's useful for this project

**1. ⚠️ This is our first real base rate, and it's sobering.** Median conversion is **0.62%** — six
paying readers per thousand. Every operator interview we've studied is a top-decile outcome. This
number should anchor every revenue projection we build in Phase 5.

**2. Vertical choice dominates list size — now quantified.** Same 5,000-subscriber list earns
~$13,600 in investing or ~$1,260 in travel. This is the same conclusion the Poyar/Imran comparison
suggested (~$17 vs ~$0.15 per audience member per year), **now supported by thousands of
publications instead of two anecdotes.** This is the strongest evidence yet for the Track A vs
Track B rubric, and it should be weighted heavily.

**3. ⚠️ A direct warning for the AI niche.** AI has **13.33% monthly churn — ~7.5 month lifetime**,
among the worst measured, because free alternatives appear constantly and erode the paid value
proposition. Given the domain edge under consideration is AI/GTM/B2B software, **this is the single
most important caution in the report for us.** It does not rule the niche out, but it argues that
an AI-adjacent newsletter should probably monetize through advertising, consulting, or community
rather than lean on paid subscriptions — which is precisely Poyar's actual model.

**4. Technology has the *lowest* top-decile conversion (5.55%).** Even the best tech newsletters
convert far below the best finance ones. Combined with point 3, the picture for B2B tech is:
good advertising rates, weak subscription economics.

**5. Retention beats acquisition, and nobody does it.** Cancel flows recover 10-20%, dunning
recovers 20-40%, and the report says few publishers run either. For a small operator these are
cheap, one-time setup items with compounding returns — the kind of thing that fits a 5-10 hr/week
budget better than producing more content does.

**6. "Free earns attention, paid earns money" is corroboration.** This is the same line Poyar drew
independently ("if they are applying the lessons, they should pay for that"). **Two independent
sources, same structural principle** — free for casual consumption, paid for decision-grade work.

**7. Six weeks to launch paid is a testable claim, not a law.** It contradicts Poyar, who waited
**4.5 years** and did fine. The report has an obvious interest in creators turning on monetization
early. Worth noting both and deciding deliberately in Phase 5.

## Caveats

- **⚠️ This is a marketing asset with real data inside it.** beehiiv sells paid-subscription
  infrastructure. The report's conclusion — that you should launch a paid newsletter, soon, on
  beehiiv — is the product pitch. Individual benchmarks are usable; the narrative is not neutral.
- **⚠️ Selection bias runs deep.** The population is beehiiv publications that *chose to launch a
  paid tier.* Not newsletters generally, not all platforms, and not the ones that never monetized or
  quit. The denominator excludes the failures, so even the sobering 0.62% median is probably
  optimistic for the wider population.
- **⚠️ The "85% of creator revenue is subscriptions" claim is almost certainly a measurement
  artifact and directly contradicts our other sources.** Poyar's advertising is 5-6x his
  subscriptions; Imran's brand deals are ~70% of revenue. The likely explanation: **beehiiv can only
  see revenue that flows through beehiiv.** Direct sponsorships negotiated over email and invoiced
  by the creator are invisible to the platform. **Do not carry this number forward** — but do carry
  the lesson that platform-reported revenue mix systematically undercounts off-platform deals.
- **The LTV math is crude and they admit it.** Lifetime = 1 ÷ average monthly churn assumes a
  constant hazard rate. Real subscription churn is front-loaded, so these are steady-state
  approximations, not cohort survival. Treat the *relative* spread between verticals as more
  reliable than any absolute LTV figure.
- **Thin verticals are noisy.** Sports = 158 publications vs business = 630+. The report flags this;
  top-decile figures from small verticals should be treated as indicative only.
- **$35M for 2026 is a projection**, not a result — and it's the number used in the email subject
  line that surfaced this article.
- **Several featured launches are not self-made audiences.** The Nerve's 1,000 members in week one
  came from Carole Cadwalladr's existing following; Ajenda started from 181,000 free subscribers and
  a national TV career. These are audience *migrations*, presented alongside organic-growth advice.
- **Every quoted expert is either a beehiiv employee or a beehiiv customer.** Four of the eight
  named voices are beehiiv staff.
