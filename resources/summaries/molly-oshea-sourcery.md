## ⚠️ Why this one matters more than its size suggests

Every other case in this research was built by someone **full-time**, or with a large team, or after
an exit. **Sourcery was built at 4:30am alongside a demanding full-time investing job — and its
founder then deliberately decided to keep it that way, and wrote down exactly why.**

> "With a ton of very early 4:30am mornings & late nights **creating all of this Sourcery outside of
> my day job**, I can say it was worth it."

> "I realized along the way just how hard it is (shocking), **as one person, to constantly pump out
> thoughtful, well-researched pieces. And on top of that, with a full time investing job, it's just
> overall very difficult to manage.** Truthfully, as fun as it sounded, I wasn't sure if I had the
> guts to take it full time, or if that was something I even wanted to do with a 'media company' —
> **and I learned it isn't.**"

Her three stated reasons for not going full-time:
1. **"I am not rich & need *real* income"**
2. **"My personal risk tolerance isn't there yet"**
3. **"I just love investing too much"** — the team, the research, the networking, the perspective

**This is the only case in the research where someone reached a real decision point about going
full-time and chose not to, with reasoning attached.** [L14](../../LEARNINGS.md) says every success
had an unpriced cushion; O'Shea is the counter-example who names the absence of one as the reason
she stayed part-time.

## The trajectory

- **September 2019:** started as an **internal newsletter** — not a public product.
- **October 2020:** publicly debuted on Substack.
- **Early 2024:** ~**6,000 subscribers**, built over roughly four years while working at various VC
  firms.
- **February 2024:** relaunched with a podcast, aiming to "grow a media empire."
- **February 2025 (one year on):** **4.5M+ impressions, ~2x subscriber count, 39 interviews
  completed**, audience grown across LinkedIn and YouTube. She also left institutional VC after six
  years for a family-office role during the same period.
- **August 2026 (inspected live):** the archive shows **exclusive CEO-level access** — Sir Peter Beck
  (Rocket Lab), Nikesh Arora (Palo Alto Networks), Josh Kushner (Thrive), AppLovin's CEO and CTO,
  BlackRock's Tony Kim, Cognition's Scott Wu, Colossal Biosciences. Plus a podcast, YouTube, and
  merch.

**Roughly four years of slow build before the relaunch that produced the access.** Consistent with
[L13](../../LEARNINGS.md) across every case.

## The business model

**Subscriptions:** **$165/yr or $15/mo** — a *permanent 70% discount* from the previous price,
explicitly to make it more affordable. **She also kept an "expense it" tier** for fund managers whose
employers pay — **the same mechanic Lenny uses**, and now a two-source pattern for professional
audiences.

**Advertising:** launched only once the audience was established — *"With our growing audience, we're
excited to announce that Sourcery is now offering ad placements."*

**Partnerships:** a deal with **Passes** giving her an LA podcast studio, early-release distribution,
and subscriber Q&A features. Podcast production outsourced to a professional producer (Matt
Marlinski, The Manhattan Lab).

**The audience is the asset, and she says so explicitly.** Sourcery is read by leaders at *"a16z,
Founders Fund, Sequoia, USV, JP Morgan Chase, Blackstone, SpaceX, Ramp, OpenAI, FirstMark, Carta,
MongoDB, Snowflake, Brex, Stripe, Anduril…"* — and:

> **"We successfully helped companies featured in the newsletter secure millions in funding, acting
> as a true 'sourcerer' in their fundraising efforts."**

**⚠️ And the same media→capital path Stebbings ran:**
> "My **deal flow has elevated with the pod** & it would be nice to have somewhere to put it all 👀…
> I wouldn't necessarily be opposed to a **Sourcery Fund** in the coming years."

**Reputation effects:** she won the Pirate Wires "Pirate Idol" contest, earning a recurring spot on
their podcast — a distribution channel acquired by competing rather than by pitching.

Her own summary of the lesson:
> **"Just go do the thing."** Being an active player, growing your own personal brand, putting
> yourself out there, **cold outreaching to people you admire**, can truly benefit you more than
> anything else.

## What's useful for this project

**1. ⚠️ It is the first credible answer to [C4](../../LEARNINGS.md) — can this be done alongside a
full-time job?** Yes: ~6,000 subscribers over four years, then 2x and 4.5M impressions in the fifth,
at 4:30am. **But the honest reading is that it took four years to get to 6,000, and she describes the
year of real momentum as the hardest of her life.** Poyar is the other data point (4.5 years while
employed) and the two agree closely on the timeline.

**2. A small audience in a high-commercial-intent niche buys extraordinary access.** ~6,000 readers —
tiny next to Morning Brew's 4M or Lenny's 1M — yet the readership list is a who's-who of venture, and
it now lands sitting CEOs of multi-billion-dollar public companies. **This is [L1](../../LEARNINGS.md)
and [L2](../../LEARNINGS.md) in their sharpest form: who reads it matters more than how many.**

**3. It validates D1 from the small end.** Everything else supporting D1 was a large business. This
shows the commercial-intent principle working at a scale a part-time operator could actually reach.

**4. The "expense it" tier is now a two-source pattern** (with Lenny). For a professional audience,
a higher-priced tier for people whose employer pays is close to free money — same product, different
willingness to pay.

**5. Cold outreach again.** Stebbings' 53 emails to Benioff, Sam Parr's brute-forced speakers,
Dwarkesh's cold emails to scholars, and now O'Shea's *"cold outreaching to people you admire."*
**Four sources.** It is consistently the mechanism by which people without networks acquire access.

**6. Monetization sequenced late and deliberately.** Internal newsletter → public → four years →
podcast relaunch → *then* advertising. And she **cut** her subscription price by 70% rather than
raising it. **A useful counterweight to beehiiv's "launch paid at six weeks."**

**7. Outsource production, keep the judgment.** She hired a professional podcast producer while
continuing to do the research and interviews herself — the same shape as Senra taking distribution
and ad sales from Colossus while refusing to let anyone touch the content.

## Caveats

- **⚠️ No revenue figures exist publicly**, and no current subscriber count. The last public number is
  ~6,000 from early 2024, and "~2x" in Feb 2025 implies roughly 12,000 — **an inference, not a
  disclosure.** We do not know whether Sourcery earns meaningful money.
- **⚠️ The 70% price cut is ambiguous evidence.** She frames it as making the product accessible; it
  could equally indicate conversion was weak at the higher price. **Nothing available resolves this,
  and it would be one of the more informative facts about the business.**
- **She is a professional VC investor.** The deal flow, the access to founders, and the credibility
  with GPs and LPs come substantially from the day job. **The newsletter and the career are mutually
  reinforcing** — this is not a cold start, and the model may not separate from the job.
- **The Brick by Brick episode was not listened to** — only its title used. If a fuller account of
  the 0→6k period matters, that episode is the obvious next source.
- **Survivorship still applies**, though less acutely: she is candid about difficulty and about
  declining to go full-time, which is the opposite of the usual retrospective.
- **Grokipedia appeared in search results and was deliberately not used** — AI-generated reference
  content is not a citable source for this project.
