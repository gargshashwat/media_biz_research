## Your notes from ~2025 (preserved verbatim in substance)

> - Media empires can influence millions and derive massive business outcomes
> - Start from a personal brand and purpose statement (change my LI to this. Delete FB, insta. Make new X.)
> - **Attention → Retention → Monetization**
>   - Attention: LI, X, TikTok, Insta, YouTube, Reddit
>   - Retention: newsletter, podcast, webinar, community
>   - Monetize: events, services, courses, paid communities. For him the main is the 20VC fund and brand sponsors
> - Focused on podcast. Built relationships and brand via podcast
>   - interviews with great people was key as people then scrambled to hear his podcast — often people not usual guests on other podcasts
>   - asked everyone after doing podcast with them — **"who are 3 other people I can talk to? can you intro me to them?"**
> - Now he has a 15 person team managing his media
> - Started a VC fund on the back of his reputation from podcast
> - Content sources:
>   - podcast is core
>   - day to day short reflections on LI and X — from guests or personal or even life updates
>   - short-form content pulled from podcast which are linked to original long-form podcast
> - Content is long-term game

## What's useful for this project

**1. ⚠️ The Attention → Retention → Monetization frame is the most useful organizing model we've
encountered, and it retro-fits every case we've studied.**

| Layer | Purpose | Examples from our research |
|---|---|---|
| **Attention** | Rented, algorithmic, discoverable | Poyar's LinkedIn (110k), Rowan's Twitter, Imran's TikTok, Morning Brew's campus pitches |
| **Retention** | Owned, direct, durable | Every single case ends up here — newsletter, podcast, community |
| **Monetization** | Sold to the retained audience | Poyar's ad packages + consulting, Fuller's adjacent businesses, Stebbings' fund |

**This should probably become the spine of the Phase 5 strategy document.** It explains why "own your
audience" keeps recurring: the retention layer is the only one you control, and it's the only one you
can monetize against.

**2. The guest-referral loop is a concrete compounding mechanic.** *"Who are 3 other people I can
talk to? Can you intro me to them?"* — asked after every recording. **This is how a podcast bootstraps
a network from zero**, and it's the mechanism Dwarkesh's case implies but never states. For someone
without a network, it may be the single most important tactic in the notes.

**3. Booking unusual guests, not just famous ones.** People "not usual guests on other podcasts" —
which matches Dwarkesh's finding that obscure guests outperformed CEOs. **Two sources.**

**4. Media → capital is the highest-leverage adjacent business anyone in this research has found.**
Fuller bought magazines and sold real estate and aircraft financing; Stebbings converted reputation
into fund management fees. **Worth understanding properly** — but note it's specific to
finance-adjacent niches where credibility itself is the product.

**5. Repurposing structure:** long-form podcast as the core asset → short-form clips pointing back to
it → daily short reflections on LI/X. One production, three distribution surfaces. **Directly
relevant to the capacity constraint.**

## Caveats

- **⚠️ I could not verify any of this.** The Loom is inaccessible to me; these are the user's notes
  from a third party's breakdown. **Nothing here should be treated as sourced fact.**
- **⚠️ It's an interpretation, not a primary account.** "notus" is analyzing Stebbings from outside.
  The Attention→Retention→Monetization framing may be the analyst's model rather than Stebbings'.
- **A 15-person team** is stated in the notes. Whatever the playbook produces now, it is not being run
  by one person in evenings and weekends.
- **Stebbings started at ~18 and full-time**, over roughly a decade, in a pre-saturation podcast market.
- **The fund is the monetization** — which requires being fundable, in an industry where reputation
  converts to capital. That path does not generalize to most niches.
- **The personal-branding advice in the notes** ("delete FB, insta, make new X") is generic
  positioning advice, unattributed and unevidenced. Treat as prompt, not prescription.
