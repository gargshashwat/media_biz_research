## The revenue model

| Line | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Paid newsletter** | **$15/mo or $150/yr**, plus an **"I Can Expense It" tier at $300** and group/team plans |
| Conversion | **377k free → 18k paid ≈ 4-5%** (he has cited 4-8%) |
| Newsletter revenue | ~$2.26M gross; **~$1.97M after Substack's 10% and Stripe's 3%** *(author's estimate)* |
| **Podcast** | Launched June 2022, ad-supported. **Lenny said it would outearn the newsletter in its first year.** Estimated ~$2M |
| **Job board** | Premium pricing; a portion donated to tech non-profits. Plus a **talent collective** matching vetted community members to companies |
| **Angel investing** | Deal access generated by the audience; returns unknown |
| **Total** | **Estimated $4M+/year minimum** from newsletter + podcast + job board alone |

**⚠️ The single most important line here: the podcast — sold to advertisers — outearns a ~$2M
paid-subscription business.** Lenny is *the* paid-newsletter success story, and even for him,
advertising beats reader revenue. This is a strong additional confirmation of **[L4](../../LEARNINGS.md)**.

Also notable: **he is still the only full-time employee**, using contractors and freelancers mainly
for podcast production.

## How the free list was built

### The four levers, in order of impact
**1. Medium first, then harvest it.** His first post — *"What Seven Years at Airbnb Taught Me About
Building a Business"* — got **~30,000 claps** and **Brian Chesky shared it with the entire company.**
He wrote six more. Then he went **back to every Medium post and added a line at the bottom pointing
to the newsletter** — that produced his first ~100 subscribers.

**2. Guest posts → the first 1,000.** Andrew Chen (now a16z) told him to publish an idea on *his*
site instead of Lenny's. Around the same time he wrote for First Round Review. **Those two guest
posts took him to 1,000 subscribers.**

**3. ⚠️ Two enormous research projects drove 50% of his first-year subscribers.**
- *Marketplace research (Nov-Dec 2019):* **interviewed ~25 people across 17 marketplace companies**
  (Uber, DoorDash, Zillow, Eventbrite, Thumbtack…), published as a **4-part series**. Added ~2,000
  subscribers **when he only had 2,800 — it nearly doubled the list**, and visibly changed the slope
  of his growth curve afterwards.
- *"How to get your first 1,000 users":* a month of research — interviews, scouring existing
  material, and asking Twitter. **Added 5,000+ subscribers in two weeks.**

> **His stated motto: "Keep writing great stuff every week, and work on something epic in the
> background."**

**4. Substack Recommendations — the discontinuity.** Launched **12 April 2022**. Within a year
**78% of new subscribers and 11% of paid** came from other Substacks recommending him; **1,000+
newsletters now do.** He went from **111,000 (April 2022) to 377,000 in under a year.**
**⚠️ The deep dive explicitly sets this aside as unreplicable** and focuses its analysis on the first
100k.

### The Twitter flywheel — one counterintuitive detail
**He gives away the entire substance in the thread rather than teasing it.** The reasoning: if you
beat around the bush, people don't click at all. He also posts **multiple different threads on the
same article** (once, two in a single day, the second getting double the engagement), **tags every
contributor** so they amplify, and **crowdsources data on Twitter** that becomes raw material for
future reports — which also pulls the named companies into the replies.

## The paid launch — the most copyable sequence in the research

**Launched 7 April 2020, after nine months of writing free.**

1. **Seeded with influencers the night before** — emailed Li Jin, Nathan Baschez, Andrew Chen and
   James Beshara to let them know it was coming and ask for support.
2. **Two weeks' notice to readers** in the launch email, answering who/what/when/where/why/how —
   creating runway to remind people before the switch.
3. **A launch thread**, then **replied personally to every single person who tweeted that they'd
   signed up.**
4. **A 48-hour launch discount**, with a next-day reminder that it was ending.
5. **The first reply he got to the launch email was hostile.** *"Make sure you have thick skin when
   you're going to launch anything, especially something people have been enjoying for free."*

**Results after six weeks: 450 paying, 13,000 free (~3.5%).** And notably, **free subscriptions rose
meaningfully when he added the paid option** — consistent with beehiiv's finding that launching paid
doesn't damage the free list.

### ⚠️ He started small on purpose
At launch, paid was **only some content behind a paywall.** No community, no podcast, no AMAs. He
mentioned a future community but didn't build it immediately — *"this helped him get his bearings and
learn how to run a paid newsletter before adding all the bells and whistles."*

## What the paid tier became (and why people stay)

- **Slack community** — grew 5k → 10k members in a year; **75 location-based groups; 193 real-world
  meetups across 30 countries in one year**; mentorship program, mastermind groups, book clubs, AMAs,
  free workshops.
- **AMAs with industry leaders** (e.g. Casey Winters), promoted at the top of relevant podcast episodes.
- **A vetted directory of product coaches.** The framing does the work: coaches cost thousands, so
  picking the wrong one is far more expensive than the $150 subscription.
- **A recruiter directory** and **100+ software discounts**.
- **20% off job postings** — pays for the subscription outright if you post one.
- **"Community Wisdom"** — a staffer reads the Slack each week and mails the best threads to paid
  subscribers, so busy people don't have to keep up. *(The author calls this the smartest single
  mechanic; it converts an overwhelming firehose into a delivered benefit.)*
- **Recognition mechanics:** NFTs of the 40 most helpful members; stickers mailed out.

### Conversion and retention tactics
- **Keep the free content genuinely excellent** — it's the only thing most of 377k people ever see,
  and it does all the marketing.
- **Promote paid-only posts on Twitter with a real summary**, then paywall on click. Documented cases
  of people subscribing directly off this.
- **Choose deliberately what goes free vs paid each month.**
- **A welcome email so effective he says many people copy it verbatim.**
- **Continuously add to the paid tier** — each addition is a fresh, non-salesy reason to talk about it.

## ⚠️ The finding that argues against our own D1

> "**If I followed the classic advice of picking a narrow niche, I'd have focused on just product
> management. But I don't care about product management that much.** I'm interested in the wider
> space of building products, helping them grow, improving your career, and building startups. So I
> kept my focus areas broad and went where my interests took me."
> — Lenny, on hitting 1M subscribers

**He deliberately rejected narrow niching, and the deep dive argues this is a primary reason he
lasted six years** — he wouldn't still be publishing if he'd boxed himself into PM alone.

His growth summary is equally awkward for tidy tactical advice:

> "**I've tried everything — paid ads, SEO, biz dev — and none of it really did a damn thing. Word of
> mouth was the biggest lever.**"

---

## What's useful for this project

**1. ⚠️ It sharpens D1 rather than breaking it — but the tension is real and should be recorded.**
Lenny stayed broad *within a high-commercial-intent domain*. "Product, growth, careers and startups"
is wider than product management but every one of those readers can turn the content into money, and
advertisers want them. **D1's variable (commercial intent) survives; a naive reading of D1 as
"niche down as far as possible" does not.** Sustainability matters: a niche you don't care about is
one you quit.

**2. The paid-launch sequence is directly copyable** and costs nothing: seed a handful of respected
people the night before, give two weeks' notice, run a 48-hour discount, reply to everyone who signs
up. **Start with paywalled content only and add the community later.**

**3. Two posts drove 50% of first-year subscribers.** Content follows a power law as sharply as
anything else. **"Good every week, epic in the background"** is probably the most important operating
principle in the entire research for a capacity-constrained operator — it means the weekly cadence is
maintenance, and the *occasional* deep artifact is what actually compounds. It also reconciles
[C2](../../LEARNINGS.md): cadence and depth aren't competing, they're different jobs.

**4. Guest posts appear on both sides of his story** — they got him his first 1,000 subscribers, and
now **8 of his top 10 posts per category are guest posts.** He still runs **5-6 drafts** with each
guest writer, so it's editing leverage, not free content. **This is the clearest capacity mechanic
we've found.**

**5. His conversion is 4-5% against a platform median of 0.62%** — about 7x. Confirms he's a
top-decile outcome and that [L11](../../LEARNINGS.md)'s base rate is the right anchor for planning.

**6. The paid tier's best perks are curation and access, not content.** Coach directory, recruiter
directory, discounts, a weekly digest of the community. **Almost none of it is writing** — which
matters enormously when writing time is the constraint.

## Caveats

- **⚠️ Revenue figures are the author's estimates**, derived from public subscriber counts and
  pricing, not Lenny's disclosures. The $4M+ total is arithmetic, clearly labelled as such upstream.
- **⚠️ Data is stale in layers.** The paid deep dive is Dec 2024 republishing April 2023 numbers;
  he has since passed **1M subscribers**. Conversion rates and revenue today are unknown.
- **⚠️ Substack Recommendations drove 78% of growth and is not replicable off-platform.** The deep
  dive says so explicitly. Any lesson drawn from his post-2022 curve is a lesson about Substack's
  network effects, not about him.
- **The starting position, again.** Seven years at Airbnb, a first post amplified by Brian Chesky
  company-wide, and Andrew Chen (a16z) personally mentoring the launch and hosting the guest post
  that produced his first 1,000 subscribers. [L14](../../LEARNINGS.md) holds.
- **Secondary source.** Growth In Reverse's product is inspiring deep dives about people who won;
  structurally survivorship-biased, though unusually honest about it — *"there is only one Lenny
  Rachitsky... you won't be able to grow like he has."*
- **The word-of-mouth conclusion is unsatisfying and probably correct.** Every documented tactic here
  sits underneath a growth engine he attributes to quality and sharing, which is not a tactic anyone
  can execute on schedule.
