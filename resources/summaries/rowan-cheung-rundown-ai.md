## What the source adds

### Scale and positioning
- **~2 million subscribers, nearly 50% open rate** across their newsletters.
- Positioning: **"the go-to person for no-noise AI news"** — the problem he identified was that
  people saw viral AI tools, tried them once, found no use, and churned. **The gap wasn't news, it
  was relevance:** why does this matter for an ordinary tech worker.

### Origin — no credentials at all
- **College dropout**, ~3 years before recording. Realized in a psychology lecture that he doesn't
  learn in formal settings; self-taught coding, design, and content via "YouTube University."
- **The ChatGPT moment:** a day or two after ChatGPT's public launch he wrote a thread on what he
  was exploring. **It went viral.** He expected the virality to die down; it increased instead. He
  dropped out and committed to **a thread every single day.**
- Then: *"How am I going to grow a business off this? The easiest thing was just starting an email list."*
- On getting Mark Zuckerberg for the Llama 3.1 interview: *"Who is this complete zero-subscriber guy?
  ... The reason I got that interview is because I'm the newsletter guy. I'm not a journalist. I
  never went to journalism school."*

### ⚠️ The human differentiation thesis — his central argument
Competitors used AI to write AI newsletters. He deliberately did the opposite: **put faces behind it,
labelled it human-generated, kept the takes visibly human.** AI is used for structuring and some
research, never the human part.

> **"Content as a whole is going to have two sides. The Walmart and the Whole Foods."** Walmart =
> AI-generated, accessible, personalized, addictive, *and not bad content*. Whole Foods = people who
> want to know the human behind it. **"The 1% that really have the humanity are going to have even
> more influence... But I think everything else in the middle dies."**

### The writing workflow (the tactic from your notes, in full)
1. Ask yourself: **do I enjoy talking or writing?** Pick one medium and start there. He is a writer,
   so he started on Twitter.
2. **If you're a talker:** use Whisper Flow or similar transcription, go on a two-hour walk, ramble
   raw ideas. Umms and ahs don't matter.
3. **Feed those raw thoughts into a Claude project trained on your own best-performing posts.** He
   exported his Twitter data, picked his ~20 best tweets, and trained the project on his voice.
4. Output is **"a draft that's 90% ready."** His framing: *"this is AI-assisted writing but it all
   stems from humanity — my ideas, and it knows exactly how I write."*

He runs the same pattern for **ad copy** — a Claude project trained on their best-performing ad
copy, generating drafts they then fully revise.

### Why Twitter specifically for AI
**Research breakthroughs, demo videos, and papers get published on Twitter and nowhere else.** For
an AI niche, presence there isn't a distribution choice, it's how you keep up at all.

### Scaling into video without doing video
~A year before recording, his audience pushed him toward video. He works 80 hours/week and couldn't.
Solution: **cloned his face and voice with AI**, scripted from his real writing, then added B-roll
and real editing for storytelling.
**Result: the Instagram account grew to 160,000+ followers in a year** off that workflow alone.

**Platform hook lengths:** LinkedIn 2 lines · Twitter 3 lines · Instagram 1 line.

### "Verticalized content is blue ocean"
> "Everyone talks about verticalized AI SaaS — AI for law, AI for accounting. These are huge startups
> getting all this money. But **no one talks about verticalized content creators.** ... Nobody's
> doing AI-for-law content. And someone needs to be the guy."

He says this as an admittedly *broad* creator — i.e. recommending against his own position.

### His learning loop (the "AI habits" framework)
1. Every day at work, ask: **could AI do this?** (Sticky note on the laptop.)
2. When the answer is yes, **spend a couple of hours finding and testing the tool.** He does this
   every Sunday. Failure is fine — *"you're going to learn where AI can't do things yet, and that's
   what AI intuition is."*
3. **Share what you learn** — publicly, or just with your team on Slack. *"I find the best way to
   learn is by sharing things. That's obviously how I grew a business off the back of that."*

### On effort
> "You're going to find lucky breaks. And when you do, it's your job to just go all in on it. That
> means you might have to spend more than 40 hours a week... **the momentum is everything.**"

## What's useful for this project

**1. ⚠️ This is the closest precedent to the user's own domain edge** — AI, tech-worker audience,
practitioner rather than journalist. Worth weighting heavily in Phase 4.

**2. "Verticalized content is blue ocean" is a direct Track A argument**, and it comes from someone
who built the *broad* version and is telling you not to. It pairs exactly with the beehiiv finding
that vertical choice sets the revenue ceiling.

**3. The AI-assisted-but-human-sourced workflow is the most immediately actionable thing we've found
for the capacity constraint.** Voice-ramble → Claude project trained on your own best work → 90%
draft. It attacks the exact bottleneck (writing time) without outsourcing judgment or voice. **This
is worth testing early rather than filing away.**

**4. Human-authored positioning as a moat is now a two-source theme** — Fuller made the same
argument ("humans are going to want authentic content from people they trust... having trusted media
brands becomes far more important in an AI world"). Two independent operators, same bet.

**5. ⚠️ But note the direct conflict with the beehiiv churn data.** Rowan is in the AI vertical,
which beehiiv measured at **13.33% monthly churn — among the leakiest.** His model resolves this:
**he doesn't monetize on subscriptions.** Free newsletter, ad-supported, plus adjacent education.
That's consistent with the emerging rule that AI/tech audiences monetize via advertising, not paid
subs.

**6. Pick one medium, then clone yourself into the others later.** The AI-avatar route to 160k
Instagram followers is a genuine capacity unlock — though it depends on already having the written
substance.

## Caveats

- **⚠️ Timing was everything and he says so.** He caught the ChatGPT launch wave with a viral thread
  days after public release. That specific window is gone. The "post daily and get lucky" advice is
  survivorship reasoning from someone whose lucky break was enormous and early.
- **80 hours a week**, and he is explicit that going all in on momentum means exceeding 40. **Far
  outside our capacity budget** — the *workflow* transfers, the *intensity* doesn't.
- **All figures self-reported** in a short promotional interview with a sponsor read in the middle.
  No independent verification of 2M subscribers or ~50% open rate.
- **He is a broad creator recommending verticalization** — sensible advice, but he has not personally
  run the experiment he's recommending.
- **The AI-avatar tactic sits awkwardly beside the "be as human as possible" thesis.** He clones his
  face and voice with AI while arguing the differentiator is visible humanity. He doesn't address
  the tension; worth deciding where the line actually is before copying it.
