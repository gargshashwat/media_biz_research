# Early-stage marketing — getting the first 1,000 from a standing start

**The question this file answers:** you have a new X account with no followers and nothing to
distribute to. What do you actually *do*, day to day, to get from zero to an audience — and is the
answer "follow people, comment on their posts, and hope they follow back"?

> **⚠️ Working assumption, not a recorded decision.** This research was tailored to the direction
> stated on 2026-09-05: **write for professional investors, in a Citrini style** — i.e. **Theme 3**
> in [06-theme3-investment-research.md](06-theme3-investment-research.md). That supersedes
> [D4](../DECISIONS.md) in practice but **has not been recorded in DECISIONS.md**, deliberately,
> because it was given as an assumption to work under. Findings that hold regardless of theme are
> marked **[general]**; findings specific to an investor audience are marked **[investor]**.

> **⚠️ Research constraint on this file.** This environment's network egress policy **blocked every
> external page fetch** (Doomberg, Growth In Reverse, Citrini, podcastnotes, Substack, LinkedIn). New
> material below comes from **search-engine summaries of those pages, not the pages themselves**, and
> is marked `low-medium` trust accordingly. Repo-internal citations are unaffected. **Several
> findings here deserve re-verification against primaries when the sources are reachable.**

---

## The short answer

**No. Reply-and-engage is not the strategy, and the evidence that it is turns out to be almost
entirely vendor marketing.**

Your instinct is directionally right — with no audience you must borrow someone else's — but
commenting on strangers' posts is the lowest-yield way to do that, and it is the version that scales
worst against a 5-10 hr/week budget ([L15](../LEARNINGS.md)).

**Not one case in this project grew primarily by replying to other accounts.** The four that actually
cold-started used something else, and the something else is cheaper.

### ⚠️ The evidence-quality finding, which is the most useful thing in this file

A deliberate search for evidence *for* the reply strategy (2026-09-05) returned, on the first page,
sources including `teract.ai`, `postory.io`, `replygenius.io`, `contagent.ai`, `xaicreator.com`,
`indieradar.app`, and an iOS app literally called **ReplyGuy**. **Every result was published by
someone selling a reply-automation tool.**

The headline claim — *"an analysis of 300 accounts that grew from under 1K to 10K+ followers in Q1
2026 found 84% used the reply guy strategy"* — has **no traceable methodology, no author, and no
primary source.** The same pages assert "500-2,000 new followers in 30 days" and "100-200 profile
visits daily" with no attribution at all.

**This is exactly the pattern [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) warns about: a lot of creator-economy material
is marketing for the thing being sold.** The contrast is stark — the operators with verifiable
outcomes (Lenny, Doomberg, Citrini, Slegers, Parr, Stebbings) describe *other* mechanics, and the
mechanic with the loudest evidence base has the weakest one.

**Treat every number in this section as uncited. Do not plan against them.**

## The four routes that actually worked

Sorted by evidence quality, not by appeal.

### Route A — One public, dated, falsifiable claim that resolves **[investor]**

**The Citrini mechanic, and the only one that is native to an investor audience.**

- **August 2022:** Van Geelen worked through the KBW Regional Bank index and looked first at Silicon
  Valley Bank.
- **Late 2022:** published a thesis that SVB was **technically insolvent**, announced publicly that
  he had **opened a short**, and posted a handful of tweets laying out the setup.
- **March 2023:** SVB collapsed. **That is when Wall Street noticed.**
- **July 2023:** the `@citrini7` account was created — *after* the credential.

Source: [citrini-research.md](../resources/summaries/citrini-research.md#-the-cold-start-established-added-2026-09-05).

**Why this works in this niche and nowhere else:** markets settle claims automatically. A dated,
specific, non-consensus prediction becomes a public credential the moment reality prices it. No other
vertical in this project verifies its writers for free.

**⚠️ Three hard caveats:**
1. **This is survivorship in its purest form.** We see the person whose short landed. Everyone who
   published an equally confident thesis that didn't is invisible. **The base rate is unknown and
   unknowable from here.** Citrini does not contradict [L13](../LEARNINGS.md) — Citrini is the tail.
2. **He had capital at risk.** The position is what made the call credible. An opinion with no
   exposure is a different, weaker artifact.
3. **Being publicly wrong is the cost of entry**, in the one theme carrying real regulatory and
   liability exposure ([06-theme3](06-theme3-investment-research.md)).

**What transfers is the shape, not the wager:** publish specific, dated, checkable claims, and make
the checking easy. That is buildable at 5-10 hrs/week. **The lottery ticket is the magnitude of any
one resolution, not the practice of resolving.**

### Route B — Borrow an audience that already exists **[general]**

**The best-evidenced route in the project, across five independent cases.**

| Operator | What they actually did |
|---|---|
| **Lenny** | Andrew Chen told him to publish his idea on *Chen's* site, not his own. That guest post plus one for First Round Review took him **from ~100 to 1,000 subscribers**. |
| **Doomberg** | **~100 podcast appearances per year**, deliberately, alongside the Twitter account. |
| **Sam Parr** | Pushed posts to Reddit, HN, Facebook groups, Indie Hackers; **asked his speakers to share the posts he'd written about them**; gave **50 free tickets to influencers** for promotion. |
| **Lenny (again)** | **Tags every contributor** in his threads so they amplify; **crowdsources data on X**, which pulls the named companies into his replies. |
| **Stebbings** | Cold outreach: found Kawasaki's email in his site's source code, cited a specific page of his book; emailed Benioff **53 times**. |

Sources: [lenny-rachitsky-business-mechanics.md](../resources/summaries/lenny-rachitsky-business-mechanics.md) ·
[doomberg-cold-start.md](../resources/summaries/doomberg-cold-start.md) ·
[sam-parr-hustle-growth-in-reverse.md](../resources/summaries/sam-parr-hustle-growth-in-reverse.md) ·
[harry-stebbings-20vc-business.md](../resources/summaries/harry-stebbings-20vc-business.md) ·
[L16](../LEARNINGS.md)

**The pattern underneath all five: you engage with other accounts by *featuring* them, not by
commenting on them.** Writing about someone, quoting their data, tagging them as a contributor, or
interviewing them gives them a reason to broadcast you. A reply gives them nothing.

**This is the corrected version of the user's instinct**, and it is strictly better on every axis:
higher yield per hour, compounding (a guest post keeps working), and it builds relationships instead
of impressions.

### Route C — Volume and repurposing **[general, but persona-dependent]**

**The Compounding Quality mechanic**, and the honest picture of the first month.

- Pieter Slegers went full-time in **July 2022** and posted **at least 20 tweets/day**.
- **After one week: 4 followers** — reportedly his brother and his best friend.
- Then, one night, **~4 → ~1,500**. He calls that the start of the snowball.
- **100,000 newsletter subscribers in 14 months**; ~440,000 followers in ~2 years.
- Tactics: **reverse growth loops** (the newsletter pushes readers *back* to social — at least one of
  five Tuesday bullets pointed to his own X/LinkedIn content), **repurposing** (one post reshared
  **five times** with better captions), and **lead magnets** for email capture.

Source: [compounding-quality-cold-start.md](../resources/summaries/compounding-quality-cold-start.md) · trust `low-medium`.

**⚠️ Two problems with copying this.**
1. **The crux step is undocumented.** Nothing accessible explains what caused the overnight jump from
   4 to 1,500. Without that, it is not a playbook.
2. **⚠️ Wrong persona.** This is a large, broad, largely **retail** investing audience — the
   [L1](../LEARNINGS.md) *low*-revenue-per-reader side of the table. Citrini's $999 professionals are
   the other side. **Tactics optimized for retail reach do not automatically transfer, and some work
   directly against a professional positioning** ([D1](../DECISIONS.md), and the pricing trap flagged
   in [06-theme3](06-theme3-investment-research.md)).

**What does transfer regardless of persona: repurposing.** Reposting a winner five times is the
single clearest lever against [L15](../LEARNINGS.md) at 5-10 hrs/week. It is the cheapest tactic in
this entire file.

### Route D — The epic artifact **[general]**

**Lenny's motto — "keep writing great stuff every week, and work on something epic in the
background" — is the best-evidenced growth strategy in the whole project.**

- The **marketplace research** (Nov-Dec 2019): ~25 interviews across 17 companies, published as a
  4-part series. Added **~2,000 subscribers when he had 2,800** — it nearly doubled the list and
  visibly changed the slope of his growth curve.
- **"How to get your first 1,000 users"**: one month of research. **Added 5,000+ subscribers in two
  weeks.**
- **Together these two projects drove ~50% of his entire first-year growth.**

Source: [lenny-rachitsky-business-mechanics.md](../resources/summaries/lenny-rachitsky-business-mechanics.md)

This is already Stage 1 of [05-strategy.md](05-strategy.md), and kill criterion #2 there ("no single
outperforming piece by month 18") is the test for it. **One outperforming piece does more than a year
of replies**, and it is the only distribution strategy in the project that survives the capacity
filter comfortably.

## The platform question — and a finding that changes the plan

[05-strategy.md](05-strategy.md) Stage 1 says *"one attention platform feeding one owned list —
LinkedIn or X, not both."* Two new findings complicate the X half of that.

**1. ⚠️ X taxes the handoff to your owned asset.** Posts carrying external links are reported to lose
roughly **50-90%** of reach (more conservative estimates: **30-50%**), and the standard workaround —
**link in the first reply, not the body** — is reported still to work as of 2026. `low` trust:
sources are SEO/social-tooling blogs, none primary, and none with visible methodology.

**2. ⚠️ Doomberg — the best-documented finance-newsletter operator on X — quit the platform over
exactly this.** In **August 2023** they put the account into "lurker mode" and moved to Substack
Notes:

> *"The mere mention of 'Substack' in a tweet leads to substantial de-boosting, and we suspect many
> accounts closely associated with the site are being punished by the algorithm."*

They describe having **"built this franchise at the nexus between Old Twitter and Substack"** — and
concluded the nexus had closed. Source:
[doomberg-cold-start.md](../resources/summaries/doomberg-cold-start.md), trust `low-medium`.

**Why this matters more than a tactic:** [L7](../LEARNINGS.md) says attention is rented and retention
is owned, and the whole business depends on converting one into the other. **This is the landlord
taxing the conversion itself.**

### ⚠️ And the destination platform now has its own network effect

Substack's **Recommendations and in-app network reportedly now drive ~50% of all new subscriptions
and ~25% of new paid subscriptions** platform-wide; individual writers claim Notes drives 60-70% of
their growth. `low` trust — the platform-level figures are unattributed and the per-writer claims come
from newsletters selling growth advice.

**But note what this echoes.** [L7](../LEARNINGS.md) and the Lenny deep dive already recorded that
Substack Recommendations took him from **111,000 (April 2022) to 377,000 in under a year**, with
**78% of new subscribers** arriving that way — and the deep dive **explicitly set it aside as
unreplicable**. If the platform-level figures are even roughly right, **that mechanism has since
generalized from a discontinuity into the default growth channel**, and setting it aside is no longer
the right call.

**⚠️ Recorded as a conflict, not resolved:** on-platform network growth is *rented* audience by
[L7](../LEARNINGS.md)'s own definition — Substack owns the recommendation graph exactly as X owns the
feed. **Trading a de-boosting landlord for an aligned one is still renting.** Doomberg's move is a bet
on a friendlier platform, not an escape from platform risk, and their own framing ("the team at
Substack has been our partner from the beginning") is not a neutral read.

## What this means for an investor publication **[investor]**

The direction is Citrini-shaped: thematic, narrative-led, entertaining register, professional reader
([D5](../DECISIONS.md)). Four consequences for early marketing.

**1. Your cold-start engine is Route A + Route B, not daily posting.** Citrini's audience came from
one checkable call; Doomberg's came from a recognizable identity plus ~100 podcasts a year. **Both
are low-frequency, high-conviction mechanics.** Neither is a content calendar.

**2. Build the checkable record from day one, and make it easy to audit.** Every piece ends in a
dated, specific, falsifiable claim — which is Citrini's own stated promise (*"you'll never have to
ask 'what's the trade?'"*). Keep a public, permanent, honestly-maintained scoreboard **including the
misses.** In this niche the scoreboard *is* the marketing, and it is the one asset that compounds
without spending hours.

**3. ⚠️ Guard the persona while you grow.** The [D5](../DECISIONS.md) drift risk is sharper here than
anywhere: entertaining investing content recruits retail *fast*, and a retail free list makes a $999
professional tier unlandable later ([06-theme3](06-theme3-investment-research.md), the pricing trap;
[L1](../LEARNINGS.md), the ~100x gap). **Route C's volume tactics are the specific danger.** The
discipline question: *would a professional allocator forward this to a colleague?*

**4. Podcasts are the highest-leverage borrowed audience in finance, and the niche is unusually rich
in them.** Doomberg ran ~100/year; Citrini appeared on Odd Lots and Hidden Forces; Molly O'Shea
acquired a distribution channel by competing on someone else's podcast. **⚠️ Capacity flag:** ~100
appearances/year is a full-time job. At 5-10 hrs/week the realistic figure is **one or two a month**,
and only once there is something to point at.

## The operating plan at 5-10 hrs/week

Replacing "daily marketing" — which is what stalled the previous attempt — with something the budget
can actually fund. This slots inside **Stage 0-1** of [05-strategy.md](05-strategy.md).

**Budget: ~2.5-5 hrs/week for all distribution** ([L15](../LEARNINGS.md) — half the total). Spend it
roughly:

| Activity | Hrs/week | Route | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Featuring & outreach** — write about people with audiences, tag contributors, cold-email one person a week | **1-1.5** | B | Highest-evidence, compounds, builds relationships |
| **Repurposing** — reshare winners with new framing; one piece → several posts | **0.5-1** | C | Cheapest lever in the file |
| **The epic in the background** — banked in small weekly increments | **0.5-1** | D | Drove 50% of Lenny's first year |
| **Posting your own dated claims** — a few substantive posts, links in the first reply | **0.5-1** | A | Builds the checkable record |
| **Replying to strangers** | **0** | — | No credible evidence base |

**Three rules that fall out of the research:**

1. **Publish before you are ready.** [06-theme3](06-theme3-investment-research.md) already flags that
   "practise without publishing" contradicts nearly every case studied — Rowan, Imran, Stebbings'
   "cringe mountain," Lenny's first Medium post. **Private practice generates no signal.** Time-box
   it to two weeks, then publish regardless.
2. **Do not hire an intern for posting.** [06-theme3](06-theme3-investment-research.md) already
   settles this: **outsource production, never voice.** Rowan's whole thesis is that visible humanity
   is the moat as AI content floods in. There is also nothing to operate before product-market fit
   ([05-strategy.md](05-strategy.md), Inversion 3).
3. **Expect the first month to look like total failure.** Slegers had **4 followers after a week of
   20 posts a day**. Poyar took **18 months to 5,000** and calls it "glacial." Morning Brew took
   **two years to 10,000**. [L13](../LEARNINGS.md) holds without exception. **The previous stall was
   almost certainly a normal month one, misread as failure.**

## Open questions

1. **⚠️ Re-verify this file against primary sources.** Doomberg's own posts, the Growth In Reverse
   Compounding Quality deep dive, and the X/Substack platform figures were all read only as search
   summaries. **The X de-boost and the Substack network-share figures are load-bearing and currently
   `low` trust.**
2. **Does the X link penalty still bite in 2026, and by how much?** Doomberg's read is from **August
   2023**. Three years is a long time in platform policy, and the answer changes the platform choice.
3. **What actually caused Slegers' overnight 4 → 1,500?** The one undocumented step in the only
   documented finance cold start.
4. **Is X or Substack Notes the right rented platform for a professional-investor publication?**
   Unresolved. LinkedIn is not obviously wrong either — Poyar reached 90,000+ followers and 14M
   impressions for $0, and allocators are on it.
5. **What is the failure base rate for finance newsletters?** Widely-repeated claims ("95% fail in
   year one") trace to SEO content with no methodology. **Uncited — do not plan against it.** But the
   direction is real: finance is the most crowded category in this project's research
   ([06-theme3](06-theme3-investment-research.md)), and [survivorship](../CLAUDE.md) applies to every
   case in this file.

## Conflicts recorded, not resolved

- **Depth vs volume.** Lenny (~50 drafts per post, two epic projects) and Slegers (20 posts/day,
  reshare five times) both worked, for **different audiences**. The reconciliation offered here —
  professional readers reward depth, retail readers reward frequency — **is inference, not a
  finding.**
- **Platform ownership.** Doomberg fled X's rent-seeking *to another rented platform*. Nothing in
  [L7](../LEARNINGS.md) endorses that as a solution; it's a choice of landlord.
- **Citrini vs [L13](../LEARNINGS.md).** Three years to 258k against "the start is slow in every
  case." Resolved above as *Citrini is the tail*, but that resolution rests on unobservable failures.
