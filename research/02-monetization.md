# The path to $100k

Written 2026-08-30 against **step #6 of the Theme 3 notes** — *"first $100k revenue through
sponsorship"* ([06-theme3](06-theme3-investment-research.md)). The sizing arithmetic is general;
the prices and conversion rates used are the **investing vertical's**, so the specific numbers
travel to Themes 1 and 2 only with different inputs.

**The question $100k actually asks is: how many people, of what kind, paying how much.** Everything
below is that one equation solved three ways.

---

## Route A — sponsorship, as the notes propose

**$100k/yr of sponsorship on a weekly newsletter = ~$2,000 per issue** across 50 issues.

The only per-subscriber ad yield in this research comes from Poyar: **85,000 subscribers, roughly
$625-750k of advertising** — about **$7.35-8.82 per subscriber per year**
([Poyar](../resources/summaries/kyle-poyar-growth-unhinged-1-5m-solo.md)).

> **$100k at Poyar's yield ⇒ roughly 11,000-14,000 professional subscribers.**

That is the honest anchor, and it is a big list. Two things push the number down:

- **Poyar says he underpriced by ~50%** and prices on the advertiser's ROI rather than CPM
  ([L5](../LEARNINGS.md)). At his stated correct price the same $100k needs ~8,000.
- **His first deal alone was over $100,000** — three quarters locked in advance, closed by email
  without a call, from a partner who had been asking to buy for years. **$100k is one anchor
  sponsor, not a sales operation.**

And one that pushes it up: **his advertisers were already waiting before he had anything to sell**
([L14](../LEARNINGS.md)). That is the unpriced advantage, and it is the part that doesn't transfer.

### The packaging is the whole game at 5-10 hrs/week
Dwarkesh puts **"close to a day+ of work per ad"** once selling, vetting, scripting and re-recording
are counted ([Dwarkesh](../resources/summaries/dwarkesh-patel-progress-update.md)). Against a 5-10
hr/week budget already halved by distribution work ([L15](../LEARNINGS.md)), that settles the shape:

**2-4 sponsors a year at $25-50k each, quarterly, category-exclusive, copy written in your voice,
one slot per issue. Not ten sponsors at $10k.** Poyar's model exactly ([L5](../LEARNINGS.md)).

### ⚠️ The unresearched hole in this route
**Who is the advertiser for a professional-investor audience?** The candidate categories — data and
terminal vendors, brokers, alt-data providers, expert networks, fund-admin and compliance software,
conferences — are **a guess. No source in this project has been read on ad demand in finance
media.** Poyar's evidence is B2B SaaS; Morning Brew's is consumer. That gap is load-bearing for a
plan whose first $100k is supposed to come from sponsorship, and it is cheap to close by asking three
plausible advertisers what they currently spend.

**Second-order:** an investment publication taking money to feature anything touching a security
inherits **Securities Act §17(b) paid-promotion disclosure** — the rule that actually catches
influencers ([06-theme3, regulatory](06-theme3-investment-research.md)). Sponsorship in this niche
carries compliance load that sponsorship in Themes 1 and 2 does not.

## Route B — price, which is the lever this niche actually has

**Investing is the highest-priced vertical measured** — median **$27/month, $292/year**, top quartile
**$600/year** ([beehiiv](../resources/summaries/beehiiv-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026.md)).
**Citrini charges $999/year** and converts at only ~0.4%, below the platform median, and it doesn't
matter ([Citrini](../resources/summaries/citrini-research.md)).

At $999/yr, **$100k is 100 paying readers.** At the investing median of $292, it is **343**.

| Price | Paid subs for $100k | Free list at 0.4% (Citrini) | at 0.84% (investing median) | at 2% | at 5% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **$999/yr** | **100** | 25,000 | **~11,900** | 5,000 | **2,000** |
| **$292/yr** | 343 | 85,750 | ~40,800 | 17,150 | 6,860 |

Investing conversion: **0.84% median, 18.69% top decile**; realistic long-term target **2-5%**
([beehiiv](../resources/summaries/beehiiv-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026.md), [L11](../LEARNINGS.md)).

**Read the top row against Route A.** At the *median* conversion rate both routes need roughly the
same ~12,000-subscriber list. But the paid route has a second dial — conversion — that sponsorship
doesn't, and at a competent 2-5% the same $100k comes off **2,000-5,000 subscribers**. That is a
different business: it is reachable, and Route A's 12,000 is not, inside two years.

### ⚠️ What the paid route costs
- **Investing churn is 11.72%/month — a ~8.5-month subscriber lifetime.** $100k of subscriptions is
  not a base, it is a bucket with a hole; roughly the whole cohort turns over inside a year. Annual
  billing (now the majority of subscription revenue on beehiiv) is the main defence.
- **[L11](../LEARNINGS.md):** 0.62% is the median for publications that *chose* to launch paid. The
  denominator flatters.
- **[L17](../LEARNINGS.md):** professional investors are the canonical expense-it audience. Lenny
  runs $300 alongside $150; O'Shea kept an expense-it tier while cutting her headline price 70%.
  **$999 already is the expense-it bracket** — which is the point.

## Route C — the adjacent business (the notes' own parked idea)

Custom reports for VC/PE reach $100k with **a handful of engagements** rather than thousands of
readers, and it is the pattern every case in this project converged on ([L6](../LEARNINGS.md)).
**⚠️ Deal sizes here are a guess — nothing in this research prices bespoke research.**

It is also, by the notes' own reasoning, **later**: it is consulting, it competes for the same hours
as writing, and it requires credibility with that audience first. **Not a route to the first $100k.
It is what the first $100k buys access to.**

## Which route, and the finding that decides it

**⚠️ Recorded disagreement.** [L4](../LEARNINGS.md) — *for a professional audience the advertiser
always outbids the reader* — is the strongest-supported finding in this project, and **the notes'
sponsorship-first ordering follows it correctly.** But L4's evidence is B2B SaaS (Poyar), consumer
(Morning Brew, The Hustle), and product management (Lenny). **In investing specifically, the reader
pays more than in any other vertical measured, and the one investing case we have — Citrini — sells
no advertising at all.** L4 may simply not hold in this niche. That is unresolved, and it is the
single most consequential open question for the $100k target.

**What both routes share is the same precondition**, and it is the thing to actually work on:

> **~5,000 subscribers who are professional investors, describable by job title, plus one piece that
> clearly outperformed.**

That is the Stage 1 gate ([05-strategy](05-strategy.md)) and it is the gate for both routes. At
5,000 the paid route reaches $100k at 2% conversion and $999; the sponsorship route reaches perhaps
$40k, enough to prove the category exists. **So the sequencing question resolves itself: build to
5,000 qualified, then test both, and let the market answer L4 for this niche.**

## ⚠️ The pricing trap, restated as arithmetic
The notes' step #5 — *all free tier, aim at professional investors* — and step #9 — *paid tier
later* — are only compatible **if the free list is professional.** Every number in Route B collapses
if the audience recruited by "going viral" is retail: retail won't pay $999, so $100k needs 343
readers at $292 off a 40,000-person list instead of 100 readers off 5,000. **Composition of the free
list is the decision that sets the $100k timeline, and it is made years before the price is.**

## Timeline — what the base rates say
$100k is **not a year-one number in any case studied** ([L13](../LEARNINGS.md)). Poyar: 18 months to
5,000, "glacial," and 4.5 years before he was permitted to monetize at all. Morning Brew: two years
to 10,000. Citrini: founded 2023, ~3 years to 258k — **from an existing X following.** Against
5-10 hrs/week, **$100k belongs at months 24-48**, i.e. Stage 2 into Stage 3.

## What to resolve to make $100k reachable
1. **Does the advertiser exist?** Ask three plausible sponsors what they spend. Cheapest, highest-value
   research left, and it decides whether step #6 is even the right first revenue.
2. **What price bracket?** $999 or $292 changes the required audience by 3-4x. Decide now, launch late.
3. **Professional or retail free list?** See above. This is the irreversible one.
4. **Themes or tickers, and which jurisdiction?** Unchanged from [06-theme3](06-theme3-investment-research.md);
   both bear on whether sponsorship is even clean.
