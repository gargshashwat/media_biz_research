## Annotations against the research
*Claude's notes, kept separate from the user's thinking above. Nothing here overrides the sketch.*

### ✅ Strongly supported by the evidence

**Sponsorship before a paid tier (#6 before #9).** Matches [L4](../LEARNINGS.md) and the
[staged model](05-strategy.md). For a professional audience the advertiser outbids the reader —
Poyar's ads run 5-6x his subscriptions, and even Lenny's ad-supported podcast outearns his ~$2M
subscription business.

**Analysts last (#9).** Every solo operator studied hired **production, never judgment**. Citrini's
10+ analysts are a late artefact of a funded firm.

**Subscriber engagement and custom requests (#8).** Among the best-evidenced tactics in the project.
Lenny runs open threads and answers reader questions; Poyar tests ideas on LinkedIn and surveys
readers before writing; **The Generalist asked readers which subject to cover 18 months before
publishing it.** For deep work at 5-10 hrs/week, validating demand first isn't optional.

**Free tier aimed at professionals (#5).** Correct, and Citrini confirms the structure: broad free
list, professional paid layer. ⚠️ But see the pricing note below.

**AI automation (#4).** [Rowan Cheung's workflow](../resources/summaries/rowan-cheung-rundown-ai.md)
is the template — voice-ramble → Claude project trained on your best pieces → 90% draft. **His rule:
AI for structure and research, never for the take.**

### ⚠️ Points of tension worth thinking through

**#1 — practising without publishing.** This is the one item that contradicts nearly every case
studied. The learning in this craft comes from publishing:
- Rowan: *"there's no creator school. The best way to learn is just through your own practice"* — by posting.
- Imran: *"I was just posting random stuff. I had no clue what I was doing."*
- Stebbings' "cringe mountain": *"you learn to summit by summiting."*
- Lenny's first Medium post — the one that started everything — was published before he knew it was good.

**Private practice generates no signal**, and the risk is that it becomes indefinite preparation.
**A defensible version: time-box it hard** — e.g. two weeks to build the habit, then publish
regardless of readiness. **The Stage 0 gate should still be *20 consecutive weeks published*, not 20
weeks written.**

**#3 — an intern for daily posting.** ⚠️ Daily posting *is* the voice, and [D5](../DECISIONS.md)
makes voice and craft the differentiator. Rowan's whole thesis is that visible humanity is the moat
as AI content floods in. **Outsource production — editing, scheduling, formatting, repurposing,
research collation — not the writing or the replies.** Every operator studied drew that line, and
Senra drew it hardest: *"no one gets to touch my stuff."*

**#5 — "going viral" as a growth plan.** Viral is an outcome, not a strategy. What the research
actually shows: **a handful of pieces drive nearly everything** — Lenny's two research projects drove
50% of his first-year growth — and the reliable route is *"good content every week, something epic in
the background."* Optimize for the epic piece, not for virality.

**#7 — buying other research reports.** Worth costing carefully. It substitutes purchased information
for original insight, and the strongest finding in this project is that **firsthand, practitioner
knowledge is the moat** ([L8](../LEARNINGS.md), [L9](../LEARNINGS.md)). Bought reports may be a
useful input; they are not a differentiator, and every competitor can buy the same ones.

**#5 vs #9 — the pricing trap.** "All free tier now… launch paid later" is right in sequence but
carries a risk: **the price you eventually set is constrained by the audience you assembled.** If the
free tier recruits retail investors, a $999 professional tier won't land. Citrini's free list is broad
*because its paid product was always aimed at professionals.* **Decide the eventual price bracket
early even if you launch it late.**

### ⚠️ The word "fund"

The notes end at a research publication, but the theme title says *fund*. These are different
businesses with different requirements:

- **A research publication** — what everything above describes, and what the project's evidence covers.
- **An actual fund** — regulatory registration, licensing, compliance, custody, LP obligations.
  **None of that is within this research, and it needs qualified professional and legal advice, not
  desk research.** Citrini's founder runs Citrinitas Capital Management as a *separate* entity from
  the research firm, which is the standard structure and itself informative.

**Note also:** publishing investment opinions carries liability the user's other two themes do not —
regulatory exposure, being blamed for losses, and the reputational cliff of a wrong high-profile call.
Bloomberg reported Citrini's founder was *"shocked"* his scenario piece moved markets.

## If this theme is pursued, resolve first
1. **Publication, or fund?** They have almost nothing in common operationally.
2. **Can you be differentiated in the most crowded niche on the internet?** Finance Substacks are the
   most competitive category in the project's research.
3. **What's the unfair input?** Citrini had an X following and a distinctive analytical frame.
   Without one, this is the hardest of the three themes to enter.
