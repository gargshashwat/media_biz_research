# Media Business Research

## What this project is

A research project to understand how modern media businesses — newsletters, YouTube
channels, podcasts, short-form social — actually get built, monetized, and sustained,
and to use that understanding to pick a niche and write an execution strategy I can act on.

This is a **learn-the-playbook-first** project. The deliverable at the end of the research
phase is not a launched channel — it's a well-reasoned strategy document that says: *this
format, this niche, this monetization path, for these reasons.* Production comes after.

## Goals

1. **Understand the landscape.** How each format works as a business: cost structure,
   time to traction, audience acquisition mechanics, platform dynamics and risks.
2. **Map monetization paths.** Ads/AdSense, sponsorships, paid subscriptions, affiliates,
   products/courses, services, and audience-as-funnel. What each requires in audience size
   and quality, and what the realistic revenue math looks like at each stage.
3. **Learn from real outcomes.** Study success stories *and* failures/plateaus. Extract
   the repeatable mechanics, not the survivorship-biased anecdotes.
4. **Find a niche.** Research candidate niches along two tracks and compare them.
5. **Write an execution strategy.** Concrete enough to start: format, niche, positioning,
   content cadence, distribution plan, monetization sequence, and success/kill criteria.

## Scope decisions

**Formats — stay broad for now.** Newsletters, YouTube, podcasts, and short-form social
are all in scope. Deliberately not committing to one; the choice should fall out of the
research rather than precede it. Narrow once the trade-offs are actually understood.

**Niche research — run two tracks and compare.**
- *Track A — domain edge:* niches where I already have an unfair advantage (AI, sales/GTM,
  B2B software). Faster credibility, smaller addressable audience, usually better monetization
  per subscriber.
- *Track B — open market scan:* niches selected purely on business attractiveness — high CPM,
  weak incumbent quality, monetizable and reachable audience — regardless of background.
  Bigger upside, steeper learning curve, no built-in authority.

Score both on the same rubric (effort, personal edge, revenue potential, time-to-traction,
platform risk, durability) and compare side by side.

**Capacity — 5-10 hrs/week.** This is a nights-and-weekends project. Any strategy that
requires more than that to sustain is out of scope, regardless of how good it looks on
paper. This constraint should be applied as a filter throughout the research, not bolted
on at the end.

## Working phases

1. **Format research** — how each medium works as a business.
2. **Monetization research** — revenue models, benchmarks, realistic revenue math.
3. **Case studies** — successes, failures, and plateaus; what actually drove each outcome.
4. **Niche research** — both tracks, scored against a shared rubric.
5. **Synthesis & execution strategy** — the pick, the reasoning, and the plan.

## Open questions to resolve through the research

- Which format best fits a 5-10 hr/week budget without capping the upside too early?
- Realistically, how long from zero to first meaningful revenue in each format?
- How much does a domain edge actually shorten the path — is it worth trading away a
  better market for?
- Which monetization paths are durable versus platform-dependent and fragile?
- What are the honest base rates of failure, and what distinguishes the failures from the
  plateaus?

## Notes

- Prefer primary sources and specific numbers over generic advice content.
- Record where a claim comes from and how much to trust it; a lot of creator-economy
  content is itself marketing.
- Keep the survivorship-bias problem front of mind — study the ones that didn't work.
